PayLoop is a Soroban contract for recurring USDC payments. A subscriber signs
one **policy**: which merchant to pay, how much, how often, and a safety cap on
the total. Funds stay in the subscriber's wallet until a charge is due. A
relayer submits each charge on schedule, and the subscriber can pause, resume,
or revoke from their own wallet at any time.

<Callout type="note" title="Testnet and Mainnet">
  PayLoop runs on **Stellar Testnet** (test USDC) and **Stellar Mainnet** (real
  Circle USDC), each with its own contract and relayer. Pick the network with
  the switch on the [PayLoop](/payloop) page, or `?network=mainnet` on every
  API call. The mainnet contract has had an internal security review;
  `GET /api/payloop/config?network=mainnet` reports `audited`, and the page
  shows a notice while no independent audit is recorded.
</Callout>

## How a policy works

| Term | Enforced on-chain |
| --- | --- |
| Merchant | Charges can only pay this address. |
| Amount | Every charge is exactly this amount of USDC. |
| Interval | A charge is accepted only once it is due (one minute to 90 days). Consecutive charges are always nearly a full interval apart. |
| Safety cap | Total charges never exceed it. The policy completes when less than one charge remains. |

**Atomic first charge.** `create_policy` grants the allowance and takes the
first charge in one invocation under one signature. If the first charge fails,
for example because the wallet lacks USDC, the whole call reverts: no policy,
no allowance.

**Non-custodial.** The contract never holds funds. It holds a USDC allowance
covering what the subscriber's live policies may still charge. Every charge
consumes exactly one amount of it. Allowance left over from an ended policy
cannot be spent, because every policy is capped, and **Renew allowance**
(`refresh_allowance`) trims it.

**Permissionless charging.** Anyone may call `charge(policy_id)`. That is safe
because the policy fixes the recipient, amount, schedule, and cap. A charge the
subscriber cannot cover is recorded on-chain (`ChargeFailed`, at most once a
day) instead of failing silently. Missed periods are never back-billed: a late
charge starts a fresh period, so it is never followed by another charge
moments later.

| Action | Who signs | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `create_policy` | Subscriber | Validates terms, grants the allowance, takes the first charge. |
| `charge` | Anyone (the relayer) | Takes a due charge. |
| `pause` / `resume` | Subscriber | Stops and restarts charges. Resuming after a missed date starts a new period now. |
| `revoke` | Subscriber | Ends the policy. Final. It never fails because of activity on the subscriber's other policies. |
| `cancel` | Merchant | Ends the policy from the merchant side. Final. |
| `refresh_allowance` | Subscriber | Re-grants exactly the committed allowance with a fresh expiry (renew or trim). |

Allowances are granted for about 100 days. Long-running subscriptions call
`refresh_allowance` before then; until they do, the relayer reports the charge
as `insufficient_allowance`.

## Try it

Open [PayLoop](/payloop) on testnet. Connect a wallet, fund it with test USDC
in one click, then sign a one-minute policy. Within a minute or two you will
see the relayer's charges arrive, each with its Stellar Expert receipt. Pause,
resume, or revoke from the same page.

On mainnet, switch the page to **Mainnet** and set your wallet to Mainnet. The
wallet needs a Circle USDC trustline and enough USDC for the first charge,
which settles immediately. Start with a small amount and a low cap; you can
revoke at any time.

## Integrate with the SDK

```bash
npm install @web-soroban/payloop @stellar/stellar-sdk
```

```ts
import { createPayLoopClient } from '@web-soroban/payloop'

const payloop = createPayLoopClient({ network: 'mainnet', rpcUrl: process.env.MAINNET_RPC_URL })

// Browser: the customer subscribes from their own wallet.
await payloop.send(
  () => payloop.buildCreatePolicy({ subscriber, merchant, amount: '9.99', intervalSeconds: 30 * 86_400, cap: '119.88' }),
  (xdr) => signWithWallet(xdr),
)

// Server: gate access on an active policy.
const plan = await payloop.activeSubscription({ subscriber, merchant, amount: '9.99', intervalSeconds: 30 * 86_400 })
```

The SDK builds unsigned transactions for any Stellar wallet (`buildPause`,
`buildResume`, `buildRevoke`, `buildCancel`, `buildRefreshAllowance`), retries
only failures the network confirmed, reads policies from the contract or the
index, and verifies SEP-53 wallet signatures (`verifyWalletSignature`). There is
no public SDF RPC on mainnet, so pass your provider's `rpcUrl`.

## Accept PayLoop as a merchant

A merchant does not run any chain infrastructure. The WebSoroban relayer
submits charges, and the indexer exposes policies and payments.

### 1. Read a subscriber's policies

```http
GET /api/payloop/policies?merchant=G…YOUR_MERCHANT&status=active&network=mainnet
GET /api/payloop/policies?subscriber=G…CUSTOMER&network=mainnet
GET /api/payloop/policies/:policyId?network=mainnet   # policy + its charge history
```

`network` is `testnet` (the default) or `mainnet`. Policy ids are per network.
A network without a deployed contract returns `404`.

Treat a customer as subscribed when they have an `active` policy to your
merchant address whose `amount` and `intervalSeconds` match your plan.

### 2. Prove the customer owns the wallet

A wallet address in a request proves nothing on its own. Ask the customer's
wallet to sign a short message (SEP-53 `signMessage`, supported by Freighter),
then verify it server-side before granting access:

```ts
import { verifyWalletSignature } from '@web-soroban/payloop'

if (!verifyWalletSignature(address, message, signatureBase64)) throw new Error('Not your wallet')
```

Include a timestamp or nonce in the message and reject stale ones.

### 3. React to charges

Each policy's `charges` count and `nextChargeAt` update as the relayer
charges. Poll `/api/payloop/policies?merchant=…` and compare `charges` with
what you last saw. Failed attempts appear as charge rows with outcome
`insufficient_balance` or `insufficient_allowance`, and the relayer retries
them with backoff.

The **PayLoop merchant** template in the IDE implements all three steps as a
runnable Express app.

## Reliability

- `GET /api/payloop/stats` reports policies by status, charge outcomes, and how
  many recurring charges landed within a minute of their due time (p50 and p95
  lateness).
- A reconciliation job compares every policy and its charge history in the
  index against the contract and exits non-zero on any difference.
- Only one relayer instance submits charges at a time (a database lease), so
  extra replicas add availability without double-submitting.
